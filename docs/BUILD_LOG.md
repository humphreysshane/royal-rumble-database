# WWE Royal Rumble Database — v2

Shane's "Opta for the Royal Rumble" project. This is the rebuild against the
full spec: every record is tagged CONFIRMED / DERIVED / PROBABLE / UNCERTAIN
/ CONFLICTING / UNKNOWN, every non-derived fact carries a source ID, and
disagreements between sources are preserved and logged rather than silently
resolved. See `DEFINITIONS.md` for the exact rules this project runs on —
read that first, it governs everything below.

## Dashboard

There's a live, browsable dashboard built from this data — Events (full
entrant/elimination timelines per Rumble) and Records & Leaderboards (the 17
tracked records plus a sortable career stat sheet for all 371 wrestlers).
It's a single published page (`dashboard/page.html`) that loads its data
from `dashboard/data.json`, generated by `scripts/build_dashboard_data.py`.

**To refresh it after any database change**: run
`python3 scripts/build_dashboard_data.py data dashboard/data.json`, then
republish `dashboard/page.html` (with `dashboard/data.json` as its one
attached file) via the Artifact tool, updating the existing artifact URL
rather than creating a new one. The dashboard is not automatic — nothing
watches the CSVs — so this is a manual step after each patch/build pass.

## Running record totals (new)

Two different things were both being called "records," and they're now separated:
- `career_stats.csv` is the per-wrestler cumulative ledger — always reflects
  full history to date. This already IS the "totals growing after each
  Rumble" table.
- `records.csv` is the current all-time leaderboard (oldest/youngest/
  heaviest/lightest/tallest/shortest ever, most eliminations single-match
  and career, most wins/appearances, biggest field-composition numbers,
  top elimination rivalry).
- `records_history.csv` is the one that answers "track the record holder
  after each Royal Rumble": every time you add a new event and re-run
  `build_derived.py`, any record category whose leader or value changed
  gets a new row here, with the event that caused the change. Nothing is
  ever overwritten in this file — it's the history of the record book,
  not just a snapshot of where it stands today. Right now it only has
  1988's "initial record" baselines; it starts getting interesting the
  moment 1989 is added and something does or doesn't get broken.
- `event_dynamic_stats.csv` covers the stats that keep moving even for a
  past event purely because time keeps passing — how many 1988 entrants
  are now deceased (currently 4), how many eventually made the Hall of
  Fame (currently 9, vs. 0 who were already inducted at the time, since
  the Hall of Fame didn't exist until 1993). These get recomputed against
  today's date every run, not fixed at build time.

With 1989 now added, `records_history.csv` actually shows real progression
for the first time instead of just "initial record" rows: Mr. Perfect's
27:58 explicitly logged as breaking Bret Hart's 25:42 Iron Man mark, Hulk
Hogan's 9 eliminations breaking One Man Gang's 6, Andre the Giant becoming
the new heaviest/tallest entrant ever (over One Man Gang), Shawn Michaels
becoming the youngest ever (over Sam Houston), and so on — this is the
"track the record holder after each Royal Rumble" feature working as
intended. `career_stats.csv` also now shows real running totals: Akeem
(One Man Gang) is up to 8 career eliminations and 2 final-four appearances
across his two 1988/1989 showings; Jake Roberts, Tito Santana, and Ron Bass
are all 2-time entrants now too.

## On video analysis

I checked the connector registry — there's no ready-made "watch WWE
footage and extract match data" tool. The closest thing available
(Roboflow) is a computer-vision platform you'd have to train a custom
model on, which is a project of its own, not a plug-in. WWE Network/
Peacock archive footage is also paywalled and rights-restricted, so a bulk
automated scrape-and-analyze pipeline against it isn't something to build
regardless of tooling.

The realistic path for the video-dependent tables (`entrances.csv`,
`moves.csv`, `near_eliminations.csv`, elimination `location_side`): you
share a specific clip or a few screenshots for a moment you want logged,
I describe what's visible and log it with a confidence level and
`human_verified` flag — which is exactly the workflow those tables were
already designed for (per your spec's own Phase 3/4: AI assists, you
verify). Not a bulk pipeline, but real data added deliberately where it's
worth the effort, rather than skipped entirely.

## Elimination-gap pass: 1991-1997 results (2026-09-18)

The Collection of Stats pass above surfaced a specific, actionable finding:
165 of 872 entrants database-wide had zero credited eliminator, heavily
concentrated in 1991 (23), 1992 (17), 1993 (25), 1995 (28), and 1997 (23) —
a stretch that predates every one of this project's dedicated fact-check
passes (which started at 1988-1992 but, per the original build plan, only
ever verified event-level facts and bios for 1991/1992, not eliminator
credits specifically). Shane's explicit decision (in response, 2026-09-17)
was to prioritize closing these gaps before building any further years.

**Method.** Five parallel research agents, one per year, each independently
required to apply this project's 2-independent-source CONFIRMED rule and to
flag rather than silently resolve any genuine disagreement between sources.
Their findings were then cross-checked against this database's own live
data before being written in — this caught one agent's false alarm (the
1993 agent flagged what it believed was a winner-mechanism error in this
project's own research brief; the database already modeled it correctly)
and resolved another's declined guess (the 1992 agent could not find
"Smash" as a competitor, not knowing this database's existing convention of
reusing wrestler_id `smash` across gimmick eras — confirmed correct, and
its one remaining gap, who eliminated him, was closed with a direct
2-source check of the database's own existing sourcing tier).

**Result: 115 of 116 gaps closed** (the 116th, Randy Savage's 1991 draw,
was never a real gap — all sources agree he never entered the match, a
storyline non-entry already correctly modeled by this database's F023). Of
the 115: 2 (Ahmed Johnson, Faarooq — 1997) needed only a small
`eliminated_by_ids` fix, since their self-elimination rows already existed
from the Collection of Stats pass but had been missed by that pass's
back-fill logic (script-ordering issue, not a research gap). The other 113
are new, sourced `eliminations.csv` rows, 9 of which are gang/tandem
eliminations (2-3 credited eliminators each): The Undertaker (Hawk &
Animal) and British Bulldog (Earthquake & Brian Knobbs) and Hawk (Rick
Martel & Hercules) in 1991; The Warlord (Hulk Hogan & Sid Justice) in 1992;
Mr. Perfect (Ted DiBiase, Koko B. Ware & Jerry Lawler) in 1993; Lex Luger,
Billy Gunn, and Bart Gunn (each Demolition Crush plus a second wrestler) in
1995; Cibernetico (Mil Mascaras & Pierroth Jr.) in 1997. These 5 events now
have comprehensive elimination coverage (28-29 of ~29 per event, up from as
few as 1 of ~29 for 1995) — see F332 for why this pass's summary-field
recompute deliberately went further than the Collection of Stats pass's
conservative "never fill a blank" rule for these 5 specific events.

**Five genuine conflicts preserved as flags (F327-F331), not force-resolved.**
